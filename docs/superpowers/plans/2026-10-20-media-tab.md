# Media tab Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** A "Media" page in the sidebar where a business keeps its brochures, price lists, photos and videos, each shown by its real content, opened in LUME's own viewer for every kind of file, and sent from a lead's WhatsApp as the real file.

**Architecture:** The file library from W3b-1 (`message_files`, the shared content-addressed store) gains previews: after an upload, a queued job sends the bytes to a new locked-down `render` service (LibreOffice, poppler, ffmpeg, sharp; no internet) that returns a content thumbnail and, for Word and PowerPoint, a PDF to view. The browser shows everything inside LUME: pdf.js for PDFs and converted Office files, SheetJS for spreadsheets, its own image, video, audio and text views. Nothing is opened in a new tab and nothing leaves the client's server.

**Tech Stack:** Fastify, Drizzle, Postgres, pg-boss, Next.js 16, motion, pdfjs-dist, SheetJS (`xlsx` from cdn.sheetjs.com, pinned tarball), sharp, Docker, Vitest, Playwright.

**Spec:** the approved canvas https://claude.ai/artifact/UUbMmh4C4Efojusnwzi64J (11 boards and its three notes: motion, "so nobody needs a call", libraries) and the owner's rulings of 2026-10-20 (memory `lume-owner-rulings-2026-10-20`): Media in the sidebar; icons, never numbers; thumbnails show the file's content; every file opens in LUME, no redirect, no new tab; keep Share by link as a per-file toggle; works on a computer and a phone; super smooth motion; crazy refined, no AI slop.

## Global Constraints

- Copy speaks as LUME, plain words, no em dashes (`scripts/check-copy.mjs`); icons, never numbered steps.
- Porcelain and Obsidian from `apps/web/src/styles/tokens.css` only; no gradients (the video controls' legibility scrim excepted); WhatsApp's own app icon (`/brand/whatsapp-app.png`) wherever WhatsApp is named.
- Motion from `apps/web/src/lib/motion.ts` (`SPRINGS`, `toMotion`): springs, interruptible, from the element's current place; every animation has a Reduce Motion form (a cross-fade); only `transform` and `opacity` animate.
- Phone width 390 px works for every screen; touch targets ≥ 44 px; no horizontal page scroll; axe clean in both themes.
- Files are read only by someone signed in with `templates.use` (archived ones: `templates.manage`); the share link (W3b-1) stays the only public way in, and only when switched on.
- The render service never reaches the internet or any LUME service; it gets bytes in and gives bytes out, one job at a time, each under a time and memory limit.
- Media works with the render service absent (a typed tile, and an honest line in the viewer for Word and PowerPoint): it is part of the standard install, not an integration, but LUME never breaks without it.
- TDD for every task; every new route in `apps/api/test/probes.ts`; every new table or column under `apps/api/test/rls-coverage.test.ts` rules.

## Review Focus

1. A 25 MB, 400-page PDF: the viewer renders only the pages near the view (and their strip thumbnails lazily); scrolling stays smooth and memory bounded. Test: `PdfView` renders at most 3 pages' canvases for a 400-page document (Task 8).
2. The render service slow, down or failing: the upload still succeeds at once; the tile shows the file's kind and "Preview on its way"; the job retries 3 times with backoff, then `failed`, and an admin can retry from the viewer. Test: Task 4 "render down".
3. A hostile file (a zip-bomb .docx or .xlsx, a PDF with 10,000 pages, a video that never ends): the render service stops it at its limits and answers 422; the API marks it `failed`; the browser's SheetJS view reads at most the first 2,000 rows of a sheet and says so. Tests: Task 1 "limits", Task 8 "rows".
4. A phone: pinch-zoom of a photo doesn't zoom the page; swipe between files tracks the finger and carries its velocity; a video plays inline (`playsinline`), never full-screen by itself. Test: Task 11 phone e2e.
5. The same file added twice (same bytes, two names): one render job for its bytes; the second row reuses the first's preview at once. Test: Task 4 "same bytes".
