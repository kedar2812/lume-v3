## What the owner sees on license.lumecrm.in

- **Fleet** in the sidebar after Clients, its badge the LUMEs that need a look or are down.
- **Health:** four tiles (Online n of m, Need a look, Down, Behind; the middle two filter), the status filter and a
  search, then every client's LUME worst first: status, last heard, version (with "update" when behind), people,
  leads, database size, disk free (amber under 15%, red under 5%) and the problems as short chips.
- A client's page gains a **Health** card: the problems in words saying what to do on their server, the database
  size and free disk over 14 days, uptime, errors in the last day, background jobs and the last backup restore test.
- **Announcements:** Live, Scheduled and Ended, each with how far it got (reached / on an older LUME / waiting, and
  how many pressed Got it); one opens to its clients; Withdraw asks first. **New announcement** (the panel's own
  sheet): Update or Maintenance (with its window), title, message, everyone or admins only, all clients or chosen
  ones, from now or later, until when, and the strip previewed in Porcelain and Obsidian.
- The bell: a disk almost full in red, anything else a LUME reported in amber; each shows again when it changes.
- Every publish and withdraw is written to each audience client's history.

## What a client's team sees (with the next LUME release)

- A strip under the top bar, **From LUME's team**: the title, the message (two lines, then More when it runs
  longer), for maintenance the window in the reader's own time, and **Got it**. One at a time, newest first; Got it
  is kept for the person on every device and works while LUME is read-only. Admins-only announcements reach only
  people who manage settings. No sound.
- Settings → About lists every announcement showing, and now says the check-in also carries this LUME's health
  (numbers only) and how many pressed Got it.

## How it fits together

- A check-in now carries `accepts: ["announcements"]`, `health` (counts, sizes and seven fixed problem keys; a
  source's name never travels) and `seen` (Got it counts per announcement).
- The licence server signs announcements into its answer only for a LUME that says it can show them: an older LUME
  gets exactly the answer it always did (it refuses any payload with a key it doesn't know).
- A LUME newer than its licence server: if its fuller check is refused (400), it asks again the old way, once, so a
  licence server rollback never costs a client its licence.
- A token may now be 16 KB in the new LUME, so three announcements in any script fit.

## Rulings made while building

1. Tokens up to 16 KB in the new LUME (three 600-character announcements in a non-Latin script pass 4 KB).
2. The web treats an API answer without announcements as none.
3. The fleet table lists clients that have checked in at least once; one never installed stays on Clients.
4. Health alerts only for a client heard from within a day (a down one has its own alert).
5. New announcement uses the panel's own right-hand sheet (the existing design scheme), not the canvas's centred
   dialog.
6. "Choose…" lists clients that have checked in; All clients still reaches the rest.
7. Got it is kept in its own table without row-level security (no lead data; counted across people by the keeper).
8. Disk free is what the API's container sees of the server's disk.
9. A maintenance window shows in the reader's own time zone (theirs, else the business's).
10. The strip folds by height, not a vendor-prefixed clamp (the web's stylesheet rule).
11. About's list of what LUME sends now includes health and Got it counts.
12. A down client's Health card doesn't repeat the silence the check-ins card shows.
13. More appears only when the message really runs past two lines.
14. A LUME retries the old body once when its licence server refuses the new one.
15. The Calendar note on a phone lines up with the cards (it had been aligned by accident and drifted with the new
    stylesheets; the phone layout now says so).

## Deferred (minor)

- The panel's announcement preview uses the owner's own clock for the window; LUME shows each reader's.
